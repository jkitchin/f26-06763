## Lecture L19 — Retrieval-augmented generation: answering from your documents
**Objectives:**
- Build each stage of a RAG pipeline and say what each choice changes.
- Write a grounded prompt that answers only from the retrieved text, cites it, and says "not in
  the documents" otherwise.
- Measure retrieval with recall@k, and trace a wrong answer back to a missed retrieval.
- Choose prompting, RAG or fine-tuning for a task, with a reason.

**Topics:**
- Why RAG: the model does not know your manuals, its knowledge is dated, and an engineering
  answer needs a source. Long context as the alternative, and what it costs.
- The pipeline: load, clean, **chunk** (by section rather than by a fixed number of
  characters, keeping source, section and page), **embed**, **index** (FAISS or Chroma in
  process; pgvector in the database from L3–L4), **retrieve** the top k, and **generate** with
  citations.
- Retrieval in one slide each: dense (embeddings) and keyword (BM25) search, and why hybrid
  search exists; filtering by metadata, such as the current revision of a standard.
- Grounding: answer only from the context, cite the chunk, and say "not in the documents".
- Evaluation: a set of 10 to 20 queries with their relevant chunks, and recall@k. Retrieval
  first, answers second; the failure chain from a missed chunk to a confident wrong answer.
- The decision table: knowledge the model lacks goes to RAG; consistent format or behavior goes
  to a better prompt, or to fine-tuning when the prompt is not enough; changing data goes to
  RAG. Fine-tuning and LoRA in one slide, so students know what they are and why they are rarely
  the first lever.

**Live demo / in-class activity:** A minimal RAG loop over an engineering standards or manual
corpus: chunk by section, embed, index, retrieve and answer "what is the minimum bend radius for
12 mm copper tube?" with a citation. Sweep the chunk size and k and watch recall@k on 15 queries,
including one the retriever misses and the confident wrong answer that follows.

**Engineering framing / dataset:** Excerpts from engineering standards and equipment manuals,
where an ungrounded answer to a compliance question is worse than no answer.

**Readings & resources:**
- Lewis et al., "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks" (2020).
- The getting-started documentation of one vector store (FAISS, Chroma or pgvector).
- Ovadia et al., "Fine-Tuning or Retrieval? Comparing Knowledge Injection in LLMs" (2023): the
  evidence behind "knowledge goes to RAG".
- Es et al., "RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation" (2023): optional,
  vocabulary for RAG metrics.

## Teaching notes / common pitfalls
- Students skip retrieval evaluation and judge only the final answer. Require recall@k on a
  gold set before any claim about answers: a good answer over a bad retrieval is luck.
- Chunking is where most RAG quality is won or lost. Fixed 500-character chunks shred tables
  and clauses; push chunking by section.
- Grounding is not automatic. Test with a question whose answer is deliberately not in the
  corpus.
- Pin the embedding model. Embedding the queries with a different model than the index silently
  ruins retrieval.
- Fine-tuning gets proposed as a way to "teach it our specs". Knowledge goes to RAG; fine-tuning
  is for behavior and format.
