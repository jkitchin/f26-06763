# Week 14 — Final Project Presentations
**Arc:** Capstone · **Lectures:** L25–L26 (two presentation days)

**Prerequisites (from prior weeks):** A finished final project — an LLM/agentic (or surrogate-integrated) system with real **evaluation** and **deployment**, built and hardened through the Week-13 studio. Recorded demo, repository, and written report are due **before** your presentation slot. Full spec and rubric: `final-project.md` (100-pt table, 30% of the course grade).

## Module learning objectives
- Present a complete AI-in-engineering system clearly and honestly to a technical audience under a hard time limit.
- Report **evaluation and deployment** results with real numbers (metrics, latency, cost) and named failure cases — not a polished over-claim.
- Give and receive useful, specific peer review.

## Lecture L25 — Final presentations, Day 1
**Format:** In-class final-project presentations. Each student/team gives an **8-minute talk + 3 minutes Q&A** (adjust to enrollment; hard-stop timing enforced). Presentation order and the day-1/day-2 split are posted in advance so every team knows its slot.

**What each talk must cover (mirrors the final-project rubric):**
1. Problem & engineering framing — what decision or task, and why it matters — ~1 min.
2. System architecture — the LLM/agentic or surrogate-integrated design, data, and tools — ~2 min.
3. **Evaluation** — your harness, metrics, the frozen eval set, and honest results including failure cases — ~2 min.
4. **Deployment** — the live API/container, with p50/p95 latency and per-request cost — ~1.5 min.
5. **Demo** — live or recorded, of the deployed system — ~1.5 min.
6. Limitations, safety/guardrails, and next steps — wrap.

**Peer feedback:** Every non-presenting student submits a short structured peer review for at least 3 talks via a provided form (one strength, one concrete suggestion, a 1–5 score on clarity + rigor). Peer scores are advisory — they inform discussion, not the grade directly — and count toward each reviewer's participation.

## Lecture L26 — Final presentations, Day 2 + course wrap
**Format:** Remaining final-project presentations, same format as L25 (8-min talk + 3-min Q&A, hard stop). After the last talk, a short (~15–20 min) **course wrap-up**: threads that ran through all four arcs (reproducibility, honest evaluation, deployment, responsible AI), where each toolchain choice pays off in practice, and pointers for continued work.

**Course wrap themes:**
- The engineering habits that generalized across data infra, ML/DL, LLM/agentic, and production: reproducible `uv` projects, tracked experiments, leakage-safe evaluation, guardrails, and deployment with measured latency/cost.
- When to reach for a surrogate vs. an LLM vs. an agent — and how to evaluate each honestly.
- Where to go next (advanced fine-tuning, distributed training, larger-scale serving, domain-specific safety cases).

## Assignment / deliverable
No new assignment. The **final project** — repository, recorded demo, written report (~4–6 pages), and in-class presentation — is the deliverable, **due this week** per `final-project.md`. Recorded demo + repo + report are due **before** your presentation slot.

## Teaching notes / common pitfalls
- **Presentation time discipline.** Rehearse the hard stop; the required advance recording is exactly why a live-demo failure won't sink a grade. Reward honest reporting of failure cases over polished over-claiming.
- **Split the roster evenly** across L25/L26 and publish the order early; hold Q&A to time so later teams aren't squeezed.
- **Grade against the rubric, not stage presence.** Evaluation rigor and a real deployment count more than slick slides; a missing hard constraint (LLM/agentic, evaluation, or deployment) caps the grade per the rubric.
- **Make peer review count.** Require specific, actionable feedback (not "great job"); it improves the room and reinforces the evaluation mindset the course has taught.
