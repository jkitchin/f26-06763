# Week 14: Final project presentations
**Arc:** Capstone · **Sessions:** two unnumbered presentation days, Wednesday 2026-11-18 and Monday 2026-11-23

**Status (revised 2026-10-25).** The final project was rescoped to fit three weeks. It no longer
requires an LLM, an evaluation harness, or a deployment: it requires a data component and a
modeling component that fit together, with an LLM as an optional third. Talks are 7 minutes plus
3 minutes of questions. The student-facing spec and rubric are `course/final-project.md` (100
points, 40 % of the course grade); this file only covers running the two sessions.

**Prerequisites:** a team project released at L17 (2026-10-28), proposed on 2026-11-04, with
slides submitted by every team on Tuesday 2026-11-17 at 11:59 pm. The written document and zip file
are due Monday 2026-11-23 at 11:59 pm, after both days.

## Module learning objectives
- Explain what the project is, how the team went about it, and what it found, to a technical audience under a hard time limit.
- Report results against a baseline with real numbers and at least one case where the model fails.
- Answer questions about any part of the project, as any member of the team.

## Presentation days 1 and 2

**Format.** Each team has a 10-minute slot: a 7-minute talk with a hard stop, then 3 minutes of
questions. A 110-minute session holds about ten teams with changeover, so split the roster
evenly across the two days and publish the order when teams are posted.

**What each talk covers** (mirrors `final-project.md`):
1. **What the project is**, about 2 min: the question, why an engineer would care, the data and the model.
2. **How the team went about it**, about 2 min: how they split the work, decisions that shaped the project, what changed when something did not work.
3. **The results**, about 3 min: the model against its baseline on held-out data, what the data did to the result, one failure case.

Day 2 ends with a short course wrap (about 15 minutes) if the schedule leaves room.

## Assignment / deliverable
No new assignment. The final project is the deliverable, per `course/final-project.md`.

## Teaching notes / common pitfalls
- **Slides are locked before day 1.** Every team submits on 11-17 so day-2 teams do not get five extra days. Present from the submitted file.
- **Ask for the failure case.** "Show us a case where it fails" is the standard first question, and the spec tells students to expect it. Teams that went looking for one answer easily; teams that did not are the ones over-claiming.
- **Direct questions at different members.** Random teams make it likely that one person built most of one part. Questions in the room and the contributions paragraph are the evidence for any individual adjustment.
- **The "how we went about it" section drifts into a roster.** Push teams toward decisions and dead ends (proposal against outcome, a plan that changed) rather than "Alex did the data."
- **The optional LLM.** Grade it only by the deletion test. A chat wrapper around a regression earns nothing in itself, and a project with no LLM can earn full marks.
- **Presentation feedback.** Two Canvas participation assignments, one per day, because the order is not known in advance. Students record team number and title for every other team that day and answer three questions (what the project asks, the main result against what, the weakest point and what to check next); due 11:59 pm that day. Questions are in `final-project.md`. Post the order with team numbers on the screen between talks. The feedback is for the instructors only and is not returned to teams; it is a second read on each talk and a check that students were paying attention.
