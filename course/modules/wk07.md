# Week 07 — Applied Engineering ML: Surrogates, Physics, Uncertainty, and Design
**Arc:** Machine learning & deep learning (signature module) · **Lectures:** L13–L14

**Prerequisites (from prior weeks):** The complete ML workflow and MLflow tracking (Wk5);
PyTorch tensors, autodiff, training loops, and architectures for tabular/field/sequence
data (Wk6); leakage-aware, grouped, and time-aware splits (Wk4–Wk6). You should be able to
train and honestly evaluate both a classical baseline and a neural network on an
engineering dataset.

## Module learning objectives
- Explain what a **surrogate (emulator) model** is, when replacing an expensive
  simulation/experiment with one is justified, and how to sample the design space to train
  it (space-filling designs).
- Build a **physics-informed or physics-constrained** model — soft PDE/conservation
  penalties, hard constraints, and monotonicity/positivity — and articulate what physics
  buys you (data efficiency, extrapolation, trust).
- Quantify predictive **uncertainty** and distinguish **aleatoric** from **epistemic**
  uncertainty using Gaussian processes, deep ensembles, MC-dropout, and quantile/conformal
  methods; **calibrate** and check the uncertainty, not just the point prediction.
- Use **Bayesian optimization** and **active learning** to choose the next expensive
  experiment/simulation, reasoning about acquisition functions and the
  exploration–exploitation trade-off.
- Integrate all of the above into a tracked, reproducible workflow — the foundation for the
  miniproject launched this week.

## Lecture L13 — Surrogate modeling, physics-informed models, and uncertainty quantification
**Objectives:**
- Design a surrogate: sampling plan, model family, and validation for extrapolation.
- Add physics to a model via soft penalties or hard constraints, and evaluate the payoff.
- Produce and calibrate predictive uncertainty, separating aleatoric from epistemic.

**Topics:**
- Surrogate/emulator modeling: why simulations and experiments are expensive (CFD, FEA,
  DFT, wet-lab), and where a cheap emulator earns its keep (optimization inner loops,
  real-time control, design sweeps, sensitivity analysis). The surrogate lifecycle:
  design of experiments → train → validate → deploy → refine.
- Sampling the design space: **Latin Hypercube Sampling**, Sobol/low-discrepancy sequences,
  and why grid sampling fails in high dimensions. Interpolation vs. extrapolation error;
  testing a surrogate *outside* the training envelope.
- Classic surrogates and when to use them: **Gaussian process regression / kriging**
  (smooth, low-dimensional, built-in uncertainty), radial basis functions, polynomial chaos
  expansion, and neural surrogates (MLP for scalar outputs, **CNN/U-Net field-to-field**
  emulators for full simulation outputs, operator-learning at a glance: DeepONet / Fourier
  Neural Operator).
- Physics-informed and constrained models: **PINNs** (Raissi et al.) — encode the governing
  PDE and boundary/initial conditions as residual penalties in the loss; soft vs. hard
  constraints; enforcing conservation, positivity, and monotonicity; and honest limits
  (stiff PDEs, training pathologies). The engineering value proposition: fewer data points,
  better extrapolation, physically plausible predictions.
- Uncertainty quantification: **aleatoric** (irreducible noise) vs. **epistemic** (model
  ignorance, reducible with data). Methods — GP posterior variance, **deep ensembles**
  (Lakshminarayanan et al.), **MC-dropout** (Gal & Ghahramani), heteroscedastic/mean-
  variance networks, **quantile regression**, and **conformal prediction** for
  distribution-free coverage. **Calibration** and how to check it: reliability diagrams,
  prediction-interval coverage probability (PICP), and negative log-likelihood / CRPS.
  Why a confident-but-wrong surrogate is dangerous in a design loop.

**Live demo / in-class activity:** Build two surrogates on the **NASA Airfoil Self-Noise**
dataset (from Wk5): a **Gaussian process** (scikit-learn / GPyTorch) with posterior
variance, and a **deep ensemble** of small PyTorch MLPs. Plot predictions with uncertainty
bands, hold out a slice of the design space to test extrapolation, and produce a
reliability diagram / PICP to check calibration. Show that the GP's uncertainty grows
where data is sparse — the property BO will exploit in L14. All runs logged to MLflow.

**Engineering framing / dataset:** Aeroacoustic noise prediction is a compact surrogate
problem with real physics (dependence on velocity, angle of attack, boundary-layer
thickness). For field-output surrogates, the alternate scenario is a 2D **heat-conduction /
Poisson** solver (generate source-field → temperature-field pairs) emulated by a U-Net —
the canonical PDE-surrogate demo that connects to PINNs. Materials option for the tabular
surrogate: **UCI Superconductivity** dataset (81 features → critical temperature, 21,263
compounds).

**Readings & resources:**
- Raissi, Perdikaris & Karniadakis, "Physics-informed neural networks," *J. Comp. Physics*
  378, 2019.
- Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell, "Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty
  Estimation using Deep Ensembles," *NeurIPS* 2017.
- Gal & Ghahramani, "Dropout as a Bayesian Approximation," *ICML* 2016.
- Rasmussen & Williams, *Gaussian Processes for Machine Learning* (MIT Press, free online),
  ch. 1–2 and 5.
- Angelopoulos & Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction..."
  (arXiv:2107.07511).
- Forrester, Sóbester & Keane, *Engineering Design via Surrogate Modelling* (2008) —
  chapters on sampling plans and kriging.

## Lecture L14 — Bayesian optimization and active learning for design
**Objectives:**
- Explain the Bayesian-optimization loop and the role of the surrogate + acquisition.
- Choose and compare acquisition functions for exploration vs. exploitation.
- Set up an active-learning loop that chooses the next expensive query.

**Topics:**
- The design/optimization problem: expensive black-box objective (each evaluation is a
  simulation run or a physical experiment), possibly noisy, possibly constrained. Why
  grid/random search and naive gradient methods are wasteful here.
- **Bayesian optimization** loop: fit a probabilistic surrogate (usually a GP) → maximize an
  **acquisition function** to pick the next point → evaluate → update. Acquisition functions:
  Expected Improvement (EI), Upper Confidence Bound (UCB), Probability of Improvement, and
  Thompson sampling; the exploration–exploitation trade-off made concrete.
- Extensions engineers actually need: **constrained** BO (feasibility from separate
  models), **multi-objective** BO and Pareto fronts (qEHVI), **batch/parallel** BO for
  running several experiments at once, and multi-fidelity BO (cheap coarse sims + expensive
  fine sims). Tooling: **Ax / BoTorch**, and scikit-optimize for lightweight problems.
- **Active learning** for surrogate refinement: query strategies (max posterior variance /
  uncertainty sampling, query-by-committee with ensembles) to spend the labeling/simulation
  budget where the model is most ignorant — closing the loop with the epistemic uncertainty
  from L13.
- Honest practice: seeding and reporting BO with random restarts (it is stochastic),
  comparing against a random-search baseline, budget accounting, and logging every
  proposed/evaluated point to MLflow so the design campaign is auditable.

**Live demo / in-class activity:** Treat a trained surrogate (e.g., the concrete-strength
or airfoil-noise model, or a fast analytic engineering test function such as
Branin/Hartmann) as an expensive objective. Run **Ax/BoTorch** Bayesian optimization to
find a design that maximizes/targets a property in ~15–20 evaluations, plot the surrogate's
mean and uncertainty and where BO chooses to sample next, and compare the
best-value-vs-evaluations curve against random search. Then run one **active-learning**
iteration: pick the highest-variance point, "label" it, retrain, and show the uncertainty
shrink.

**Engineering framing / dataset:** This is the design-loop payoff of the whole arc:
mix-design optimization (maximize concrete strength subject to cost/CO₂ constraints),
airfoil-shape/operating-point tuning to minimize noise, or materials discovery (maximize
critical temperature). Each frames "what experiment should I run next?" as an optimization
over a surrogate with uncertainty — the core intellectual move of applied engineering ML.

**Readings & resources:**
- Shahriari, Swersky, Wang, Adams & de Freitas, "Taking the Human Out of the Loop: A Review
  of Bayesian Optimization," *Proc. IEEE* 104(1), 2016.
- Frazier, "A Tutorial on Bayesian Optimization" (arXiv:1807.02811).
- Ax / BoTorch documentation: "Getting Started," service-API tutorial, and the

- Settles, "Active Learning Literature Survey," Univ. of Wisconsin–Madison TR 1648, 2009.
- Frazier & Wang, "Bayesian Optimization for Materials Design," in *Information Science for
  Materials Discovery and Design* (2016) — engineering framing.

## Teaching notes / common pitfalls
- Students conflate "the model gives an uncertainty" with "the uncertainty is correct."
  Insist on a calibration check (reliability diagram / PICP) — an uncalibrated interval is
  worse than none in a design loop.
- The aleatoric-vs-epistemic distinction is the conceptual crux of the week; anchor it with
  the GP demo (variance grows away from data = epistemic) so it is not just vocabulary.
- PINNs are seductive but finicky; for a 2-week timeline, a *soft physics penalty* or a
  hard positivity/monotonicity constraint on a standard net is a far safer "physics-informed"
  choice than a full PDE-residual PINN. Steer miniprojects accordingly.
- Extrapolation is where surrogates fail and where design loops push them. Always hold out a
  region of the design space, not just random rows, and report extrapolation error
  separately.
- BO is stochastic — a single lucky run is not evidence. Require multiple seeds and a
  random-search baseline before claiming BO "worked."
- Keep compute honest: emphasize that the whole point of the surrogate is to *avoid* the
  expensive evaluation, so students should count and report the simulation/experiment budget.
