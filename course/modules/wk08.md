## Lecture L15 — Bayesian optimization
**Objectives:**
- Explain the Bayesian-optimization loop and why it suits expensive, noisy, black-box
  objectives.
- Write and compute the common acquisition functions: probability of improvement (PI),
  expected improvement (EI), upper and lower confidence bounds (UCB for maximizing, LCB for
  minimizing) and Thompson sampling, and explain how their parameters set the balance
  between exploration and exploitation.
- Run Bayesian optimization in BoTorch (fit a GP, optimize an acquisition function, evaluate,
  update) and compare it with random search over several seeds.
- Name the extensions engineers need (constraints, batches, several objectives, cheap and
  expensive fidelities) and where BoTorch and Ax support them.

**Topics:**
- The problem: every evaluation is an experiment or a simulation, the budget is tens of
  evaluations, there are no gradients, and the measurements are noisy. Why grid and random
  search waste that budget. This is L13's "Gen 1": machine learning that chooses its data.
- The surrogate: a GP's posterior mean and variance (recap of L9 and L14), its
  hyperparameters fitted by marginal likelihood, and why inputs and outputs are normalized.
- **Acquisition functions**, each written out with $z = (\mu(x) - f^* - \xi)/\sigma(x)$:
  - PI: $\Phi(z)$, the probability of beating the best value so far by $\xi$.
  - EI: $(\mu - f^* - \xi)\,\Phi(z) + \sigma\,\phi(z)$, how much it is expected to beat it by.
  - UCB: $\mu(x) + \kappa\,\sigma(x)$ for maximizing; LCB: $\mu(x) - \kappa\,\sigma(x)$ for
    minimizing. $\kappa$ (or $\beta$ in GP-UCB) is the exploration knob, and GP-UCB comes
    with regret guarantees (Srinivas et al.).
  - Thompson sampling: draw one function from the posterior and evaluate at its optimum.
  - The trade-off on one plot: the same GP, four acquisitions, four different next points.
- Optimizing the acquisition function: cheap, many starts, gradients through the GP;
  BoTorch's `optimize_acqf`. LogEI as the numerically safer form of EI (Ament et al., 2023).
- **BoTorch**: `SingleTaskGP`, `fit_gpytorch_mll`, `LogExpectedImprovement`,
  `UpperConfidenceBound`, `optimize_acqf`; Ax as the higher-level service API on top of it.
  Optuna (L10) as the lighter tool that students have already used.
- Honest practice: several seeds, a random-search baseline, best-so-far against evaluations
  with its spread across seeds, and every evaluated point logged (MLflow), so the campaign can
  be audited.
- Extensions, one slide each: constraints (a second model for feasibility, such as cost or
  CO₂ limits), batches of experiments at once (qEI), several objectives and the Pareto front
  (qEHVI), and several fidelities (cheap coarse simulations with expensive fine ones).
- Case study: Bayesian optimization against expert chemists on reaction optimization
  (Shields et al., 2021).

**Live demo / in-class activity:** BoTorch on a 1-D test function, watching EI, UCB and
Thompson sampling choose different points as the GP updates. Then the concrete strength
dataset as an "expensive" objective (a trained surrogate stands in for pouring a mix):
maximize strength over the mix proportions with EI and with UCB at two values of κ, against
random search, five seeds each, best-so-far curves with their spread.

**Engineering framing / dataset:** Mix design on the concrete strength dataset (from L9 and
L14), with cost or cement content as the constraint in the extensions. Each evaluation stands
for a mix the lab has to pour and cure for 28 days.

## Lecture L16 — Active learning and reinforcement learning
**Objectives:**
- Define active learning and contrast it with Bayesian optimization.
- Choose a query strategy: uncertainty sampling, query-by-committee, and batches that are
  both uncertain and diverse.
- Define reinforcement learning and its vocabulary: agent, environment, state, action,
  reward, policy, return, discount factor, value function, Q-function, episode, and
  exploration against exploitation (ε-greedy).
- Implement tabular Q-learning on a small engineering control problem and compare it with a
  PID controller.
- Say when reinforcement learning is the right tool and when it is not.

**Topics:**
- **Active learning**: the loop (train, score a pool of candidates, query the most informative,
  label it by running the experiment or simulation, retrain); pool-based and stream-based;
  cold start and stopping. Query strategies: uncertainty sampling (largest posterior variance),
  query-by-committee (disagreement of L14's bootstrapped models), and diverse batches. The
  evaluation is a learning curve, error against labels, against random sampling.
- Active learning against Bayesian optimization: the same loop and surrogate, different goals
  (a model that is good everywhere, or one optimum), so different acquisition functions.
- Case study: an autonomous mobile robotic chemist that chose its own experiments (Burger et
  al., 2020).
- **From one decision to a sequence**: BO and active learning choose one experiment at a time,
  with no state. Reinforcement learning chooses actions that change a system's state over time,
  with rewards that can arrive late. Bandits as the bridge: UCB comes from there.
- **Reinforcement learning**, following Sutton & Barto and the PyCSE chapter: the
  agent–environment loop, the Markov decision process, policy π(a|s), reward and return
  $G = \sum_k \gamma^k r_{t+k}$, value V(s) and Q(s, a).
- **Q-learning**: $Q(s,a) \leftarrow Q(s,a) + \alpha\,[\,r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s',a') - Q(s,a)\,]$,
  ε-greedy exploration, on a grid world and then on tank level control, compared with PID.
- Deep reinforcement learning in one slide: DQN and PPO; Gymnasium environments and
  Stable-Baselines3; why sample efficiency and safety keep it in simulators and digital twins,
  and the gap from simulation to the real plant.
- When not to use reinforcement learning: a process model with MPC or an optimizer already
  works, there is no safe way to explore, or there is little data; reward hacking (the agent
  maximizes the reward, not the intent).
- Case studies: magnetic control of tokamak plasmas (Degrave et al., 2022) and chemical
  reaction optimization (Zhou, Li & Zare, 2017); process-control reviews for the engineering
  view (Shin et al., 2019; Nian, Liu & Huang, 2020).

**Live demo / in-class activity:** (1) One active-learning campaign on the concrete strength
dataset: uncertainty sampling with a bootstrapped ensemble against random sampling, learning
curves over five seeds. (2) Tabular Q-learning for tank level control, after the PyCSE
chapter, trained in seconds and compared with a PID controller on the same setpoint changes.
If time allows, the same environment in Gymnasium with a Stable-Baselines3 agent.

**Engineering framing / dataset:** Surrogate refinement where each label is an expensive
experiment (the concrete strength dataset again), and control problems with state: a tank
level and, from the PyCSE chapter, battery energy management under time-varying prices.

## Teaching notes / common pitfalls
- BO is stochastic, so a single lucky run is not evidence. Require several seeds and a
  random-search baseline before anyone claims BO "worked".
- Keep the budget honest: the surrogate exists to avoid expensive evaluations, so students
  count and report how many they used.
- Sign errors: UCB for maximizing, LCB for minimizing, and BoTorch maximizes by default. Most
  "BO does not work" bugs are an objective with the wrong sign.
- Plain EI goes flat far from the data and its gradients vanish; use LogEI in BoTorch.
- Active learning has a cold start: with five labels the uncertainty is not trustworthy yet,
  so seed with a small space-filling design.
- Keep reinforcement learning tabular in class. Deep RL libraries are one slide; a zoo of
  algorithms crowds out the vocabulary and the one update rule students need.
- Reward hacking is the failure mode to show, not just name: change the tank reward and watch
  the agent satisfy it the wrong way.
- Point students at the PyCSE chapter's "when not to use RL" section. For most engineering
  control problems with a good model, MPC or a tuned PID is the right answer.
