## Lecture L20 — Reinforcement learning (optional)
**Objectives:**
- Define reinforcement learning and its vocabulary: agent, environment, state, action,
  reward, policy, return, discount factor, value function, Q-function, episode, and
  exploration against exploitation (ε-greedy).
- Implement tabular Q-learning on a small engineering control problem and compare it with a
  PID controller.
- Say when reinforcement learning is the right tool and when it is not.

**Topics:**
- **From one decision to a sequence**: BO and active learning choose one experiment at a time,
  with no state. Reinforcement learning chooses actions that change a system's state over time,
  with rewards that can arrive late. Bandits as the bridge: UCB comes from there.
- **Reinforcement learning**, following Sutton & Barto and the PyCSE chapter: the
  agent–environment loop, the Markov decision process, policy π(a|s), reward and return
  $G = \sum_k \gamma^k r_{t+k}$, value V(s) and Q(s, a).
- **Q-learning**: $Q(s,a) \leftarrow Q(s,a) + \alpha\,[\,r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s',a') - Q(s,a)\,]$,
  ε-greedy exploration, on a grid world and then on tank level control, compared with PID.
- Deep reinforcement learning in one slide: DQN and PPO; Gymnasium environments and
  Stable-Baselines3; why sample efficiency and safety keep it in simulators and digital twins,
  and the gap from simulation to the real plant.
- When not to use reinforcement learning: a process model with MPC or an optimizer already
  works, there is no safe way to explore, or there is little data; reward hacking (the agent
  maximizes the reward, not the intent).
- Case studies: magnetic control of tokamak plasmas (Degrave et al., 2022) and chemical
  reaction optimization (Zhou, Li & Zare, 2017); process-control reviews for the engineering
  view (Shin et al., 2019; Nian, Liu & Huang, 2020).

**Live demo / in-class activity:** Tabular Q-learning for tank level control, after the
PyCSE chapter, trained in seconds and compared with a PID controller on the same setpoint
changes. If time allows, the same environment in Gymnasium with a Stable-Baselines3 agent.

**Engineering framing / dataset:** Control problems with state: a tank level and, from the
PyCSE chapter, battery energy management under time-varying prices.

**Readings & resources:**
- Sutton & Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 2nd ed. (MIT Press, 2018), free
  online: chapters 1, 3 and 6.
- Kitchin, *pycse*, "Reinforcement learning":
  https://kitchingroup.cheme.cmu.edu/pycse/book/26-reinforcement-learning.html: the
  definitions, Q-learning, grid world, tank level control against PID, battery management,
  Gymnasium and Stable-Baselines3, and when not to use RL.
- Gymnasium and Stable-Baselines3 documentation.
- Shin, Badgwell, Liu & Lee, "Reinforcement Learning – Overview of recent progress and
  implications for process control," *Computers & Chemical Engineering* 127, 2019.
- Nian, Liu & Huang, "A review on reinforcement learning: Introduction and applications in
  industrial process control," *Computers & Chemical Engineering* 139, 2020.
- Degrave et al., "Magnetic control of tokamak plasmas through deep reinforcement learning,"
  *Nature* 602, 2022.
- Zhou, Li & Zare, "Optimizing Chemical Reactions with Deep Reinforcement Learning," *ACS
  Central Science* 3, 2017.

**Teaching notes:**
- Keep reinforcement learning tabular in class. Deep RL libraries are one slide; a zoo of
  algorithms crowds out the vocabulary and the one update rule students need.
- Reward hacking is the failure mode to show, not just name: change the tank reward and watch
  the agent satisfy it the wrong way.
- Point students at the PyCSE chapter's "when not to use RL" section. For most engineering
  control problems with a good model, MPC or a tuned PID is the right answer.
