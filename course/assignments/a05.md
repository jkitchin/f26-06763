## The bioreactor

```{figure} figures/a05-reactor.png
:alt: A stirred bioreactor. A blue arrow labeled feed F in cubic meters per second with substrate S_F enters the top. An orange arrow labeled outflow F with X and S leaves through the right wall. Labels point to the cells (biomass X), the liquid (substrate S) and the liquid level (volume V, constant, in equals out).
:width: 75%

The feed enters at rate $F$ with substrate concentration $S_F$. The outflow leaves at the same rate, at the reactor concentrations $X$ and $S$, so the volume $V$ is constant.
```

The model consists of two dynamic mass balances, one for the biomass $X$ and one for the substrate $S$:

$$
\frac{dX}{dt} = \underbrace{-\frac{F}{V}\,X}_{\text{outflow}} + \underbrace{\mu(S)\,X}_{\text{growth}}
$$

$$
\frac{dS}{dt} = \underbrace{\frac{F}{V}\,(S_F - S)}_{\text{feed} - \text{outflow}} - \underbrace{\frac{\mu(S)}{Y(S)}\,X}_{\text{consumption}}
$$

where $\mu(S)$ is the specific growth rate and $Y(S)$ the yield:

$$
\mu(S) = 10^{-3}\,S\,e^{-S/120}, \qquad Y(S) = 5.4 \cdot 10^{-3} + 1.8 \cdot 10^{-4}\,S
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Value | Units |
|---|---|---|---|
| $X$ | Biomass concentration | state | kg/m³ |
| $S$ | Substrate concentration | state | mol/m³ |
| $\mu(S)$ | Specific growth rate | equation above | 1/s |
| $Y(S)$ | Yield: biomass made per substrate consumed | equation above | kg/mol |
| $V$ | Reactor volume | $\pi \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5.7$; in code `np.pi * 3**2 * 5.7` | m³ |
| $F$ | Feed and outflow rate | 5.02597 | m³/s |
| $S_F$ | Substrate concentration in the feed, the same in every experiment | 300 | mol/m³ |

## 1. Discrete-time model
Consider two ARX(1,1) models (Lecture 7, "The discrete model is the feature list"), one per variable. Each predicts the next sample of its variable from one lag of itself (the output) and one lag of the other variable (the input; in Lecture 7 the input was a valve):

$$
X_{k+1} = a_X\,X_k + b_X\,S_k + c_X
$$

$$
S_{k+1} = a_S\,S_k + b_S\,X_k + c_S
$$

$c_X$ and $c_S$ are the intercepts that `Ridge` fits. Together, the two models are the linear state-space model of Lecture 7 ("Model structures: ARX, NARX, state space, RNN"), with the state $(X, S)$. Both states are measured, so one lag of each is enough. Lecture 7 ("Multiple lags and the regression vector") notes that a loop with two storage terms needs more than one lag; that is the case when only one variable is measured.

## 2. PINN
Consider a network that maps time to the two states, $t \mapsto (X_\text{NN}, S_\text{NN})$. A network of $t$ alone represents one experiment: "one trained PINN is one solution" (Lecture 13, "The physics is a penalty, not a constraint"). Train it for experiment 5, from the full model and its initial condition. **It uses no measurements.** Here $\mu(S)$ and $Y(S)$ are the known expressions of the bioreactor section.

The residuals of the two balances at the collocation points $t_j$ are

$$
r_X = \frac{dX_\text{NN}}{dt} + \frac{F}{V}\,X_\text{NN} - \mu(S_\text{NN})\,X_\text{NN}
$$

$$
r_S = \frac{dS_\text{NN}}{dt} - \frac{F}{V}\,(S_F - S_\text{NN}) + \frac{\mu(S_\text{NN})}{Y(S_\text{NN})}\,X_\text{NN}
$$

$dX/dt$ is of order $X_\text{scale}/T_\text{scale}$, so the scaled residuals are of order one:

$$
\tilde{r}_X = \frac{T_\text{scale}}{X_\text{scale}}\,r_X, \qquad
\tilde{r}_S = \frac{T_\text{scale}}{S_\text{scale}}\,r_S
$$

The data loss is the error at the initial condition, the only data point; the physics loss is the mean of the squared scaled residuals:

$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{data} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{X_\text{NN}(0) - X_0}{X_\text{scale}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{S_\text{NN}(0) - S_0}{S_\text{scale}}\right)^2\right]
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{physics} = \frac{1}{301} \sum_{j=1}^{301} \left(\tilde{r}_X(t_j)^2 + \tilde{r}_S(t_j)^2\right)
$$

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{data} + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_\text{physics}
$$

Using JAX and Optax, as in `l13-pinn-jax.ipynb`, do the following:

a) Code the network below. It is the network of `l13-pinn-jax.ipynb` (`init`, 3 hidden layers of 32 tanh units) with three changes: it divides $t$ by `T_SCALE`, it has two outputs, and `jax.nn.softplus` (JAX; L13, where it keeps $\mu$ positive) keeps $X$ and $S$ positive before they are multiplied by `SCALE`. `jax.grad` (JAX; L11, L13) needs a function with one output, so there is one function per state.

```python
T_SCALE = 300.0                  # s
X_SCALE, S_SCALE = 5.0, 100.0    # kg/m3, mol/m3
SCALE = jnp.array([X_SCALE, S_SCALE])


def init(key, sizes):            # as in l13-pinn-jax.ipynb
    params = []
    for n_in, n_out in zip(sizes[:-1], sizes[1:]):
        key, sub = jax.random.split(key)
        W = jax.random.normal(sub, (n_in, n_out)) * np.sqrt(1 / n_in)
        params.append((W, jnp.zeros(n_out)))
    return params


def net(params, t):
    h = t[:, None] / T_SCALE
    for W, b in params[:-1]:
        h = jnp.tanh(h @ W + b)
    W, b = params[-1]
    return jax.nn.softplus(h @ W + b) * SCALE    # X and S, never negative


def X_nn(params, t):
    return net(params, jnp.array([t]))[0, 0]


def S_nn(params, t):
    return net(params, jnp.array([t]))[0, 1]


dX = jax.grad(X_nn, argnums=1)    # dX/dt
dS = jax.grad(S_nn, argnums=1)    # dS/dt

params0 = init(jax.random.PRNGKey(0), [1, 32, 32, 32, 2])
t_phys = jnp.linspace(0.0, 300.0, 301)     # collocation points, floats
```

b) Code $\tilde{r}_X$ and $\tilde{r}_S$ at `t_phys`, with `jax.vmap` (JAX; L11, L13) over $t_j$, as the residual of `l13-pinn-jax.ipynb` does.

c) Code $\mathcal{L}$ with $\lambda = 0.01$. Train with `optax.adam(1e-3)` (Optax; L11, L13), as in `l13-pinn-jax.ipynb`, for 20,000 steps (its `train` defaults to 30,000).

d) Evaluate the network at $t = 0, 5, \ldots, 300$ s and plot it with the measurements of experiment 5. How long did training take (wall-clock time of the training loop, compilation included)? What would you have to do to predict a sixth experiment?

## 3. Neural ODE
Consider the same balances, with the growth rate replaced by a network $\mu_\text{NN}(S)$:

$$
\frac{dX}{dt} = -\frac{F}{V}\,X + \mu_\text{NN}(S)\,X, \qquad
\frac{dS}{dt} = \frac{F}{V}\,(S_F - S) - \frac{\mu_\text{NN}(S)}{Y(S)}\,X
$$

Here $Y(S)$ is the known expression and $S_F = 300$ mol/m³, but **do not use the expression of $\mu(S)$: the network learns it.** The true $\mu(S)$ appears only in (f), to check the result.

Using Equinox, Diffrax and Optax, as in `l13-neural-dae.ipynb`, learn $\mu_\text{NN}$ from experiments 1 to 4:

a) Code the network below: `GrowthRate` of `l13-neural-dae.ipynb` (`eqx.Module`, `eqx.nn.MLP`: Equinox; L13), with $S$ as its only input.

```python
mu_scale = 0.05      # 1/s: sets the size of mu


class GrowthRate(eqx.Module):
    mlp: eqx.nn.MLP

    def __call__(self, S):
        out = self.mlp(jnp.array([S / 100.0]))[0]
        return mu_scale * jax.nn.softplus(out)    # mu >= 0


mu_net = GrowthRate(eqx.nn.MLP(
    in_size=1,
    out_size=1,
    width_size=16,
    depth=2,
    activation=jnp.tanh,
    key=jax.random.PRNGKey(0),
))
```

b) Code the balances as `balances(t, x, args)` of `l13-neural-dae.ipynb`, with two changes:
- Pass only the network through `args` (`args=mu_net`) and use $S_F = 300$ as a constant. Do not pass `x0[2]` as L13 does: there the feed concentration was each batch's initial substrate. With two states, `x0[2]` raises no error: JAX clamps the index and reads `x0[1]` (Lecture 11, out-of-bounds indexing), so $S_F$ silently becomes each experiment's own $S_0$ (10, 20, 120 and 60 in training).
- Evaluate $\mu_\text{NN}$ and $Y$ at `jax.nn.softplus(S)` (JAX; L13), not at $S$. $\text{softplus}(S) = \ln(1 + e^S)$ is within 0.01 of $S$ for $S > 5$ mol/m³ and is always positive.

Hint: Lecture 13 notes that nothing in the neural ODE keeps $S \ge 0$. $Y(S)$ reaches zero at $S = -30$ mol/m³. Without the softplus, training can drive $S$ there, and the solver stops with "The maximum number of solver steps was reached."

c) Simulate the balances with `diffrax.diffeqsolve` (Diffrax; L13), using `diffrax.Tsit5()`, `dt0=0.5` and `diffrax.PIDController(rtol=1e-6, atol=1e-8)`.

d) Train on experiments 1 to 4 for 10,000 steps, as in `l13-neural-dae.ipynb` (`eqx.filter_value_and_grad`, `eqx.filter_jit`: Equinox; L13). The loss is the mean of $\left((\text{prediction} - \text{data}) / \sigma\right)^2$ over all their samples, where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of their $X$ and of their $S$. The optimizer is Adam with a cosine schedule (Optax; L13):

