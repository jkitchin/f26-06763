## The bioreactor

```{figure} figures/a05-reactor.png
:alt: A stirred bioreactor. A blue arrow labeled feed F in cubic meters per second with substrate S_F enters the top. An orange arrow labeled outflow F with X and S leaves through the right wall. Labels point to the cells (biomass X), the liquid (substrate S) and the liquid level (volume V, constant, in equals out).
:width: 75%

The feed enters at rate $F$ with substrate concentration $S_F$. The outflow leaves at the same rate, at the reactor concentrations $X$ and $S$, so the volume $V$ is constant.
```

The model consists of two dynamic mass balances, one for the biomass $X$ and one for the substrate $S$:

$$
\frac{dX}{dt} = \underbrace{-\frac{F}{V}\,X}_{\text{outflow}} + \underbrace{\mu(S)\,X}_{\text{growth}}
$$

$$
\frac{dS}{dt} = \underbrace{\frac{F}{V}\,(S_F - S)}_{\text{feed} - \text{outflow}} - \underbrace{\frac{\mu(S)}{Y(S)}\,X}_{\text{consumption}}
$$

where $\mu(S)$ is the specific growth rate and $Y(S)$ the yield:

$$
\mu(S) = 10^{-3}\,S\,e^{-S/120}, \qquad Y(S) = 5.4 \cdot 10^{-3} + 1.8 \cdot 10^{-4}\,S
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Value | Units |
|---|---|---|---|
| $X$ | Biomass concentration | state | kg/m³ |
| $S$ | Substrate concentration | state | mol/m³ |
| $\mu(S)$ | Specific growth rate | equation above | 1/s |
| $Y(S)$ | Yield: biomass made per substrate consumed | equation above | kg/mol |
| $V$ | Reactor volume | $\pi \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5.7$ | m³ |
| $F$ | Feed and outflow rate | 5.02597 | m³/s |
| $S_F$ | Substrate concentration in the feed | 300 | mol/m³ |

## 2. PINN
Consider the PINN of Lecture 13: a network trained on data and on the residuals of the model's equations. For this reactor the residuals are

$$
r_X = \frac{dX}{dt} + \frac{F}{V}\,X - \mu(S)\,X, \qquad
r_S = \frac{dS}{dt} - \frac{F}{V}\,(S_F - S) + \frac{\mu(S)}{Y(S)}\,X
$$

with $X$ and $S$ the network's outputs, and $\mu(S)$ and $Y(S)$ from the bioreactor section. $dX/dt$ and $dS/dt$ come from `jax.grad` (JAX; Lectures 11 and 13) of each output with respect to $t$, as in the code of 2(a).

Train both PINNs with `optax.adam(1e-3)` (Optax; Lectures 11 and 13) for 20,000 steps. Use the training step of `l13-neural-dae.ipynb`, with `eqx.filter_value_and_grad` and `eqx.filter_jit` (Equinox; Lecture 13): the networks here are Equinox networks, and the step of `l13-pinn-jax.ipynb` does not work on them.

a) **A first PINN.** To predict experiment 5, the network must know where an experiment starts, so it takes the initial condition as an input: $(t, X_0, S_0) \mapsto (X, S)$.

```python
net_a = eqx.nn.MLP(            # (t, X0, S0) -> (X, S)
    in_size=3,
    out_size=2,
    width_size=32,
    depth=3,
    activation=jnp.tanh,
    key=jax.random.PRNGKey(0),
)


def pinn_a(net, t, X0, S0):
    # X and S at time t, for the experiment that starts at (X0, S0)
    return net(jnp.array([t, X0, S0]))


def residuals_a(net, t, X0, S0):
    X, S = pinn_a(net, t, X0, S0)
    # dX/dt and dS/dt: jax.grad of each output with respect to t
    dXdt = jax.grad(lambda t: pinn_a(net, t, X0, S0)[0])(t)
    dSdt = jax.grad(lambda t: pinn_a(net, t, X0, S0)[1])(t)
    # mu(S) and Y(S) are yours, written with jnp
    rX = dXdt + F / V * X - mu(S) * X
    rS = dSdt - F / V * (SF - S) + mu(S) / Y(S) * X
    return jnp.array([rX, rS])
```

Evaluate `pinn_a` and `residuals_a` at every sample at once with `jax.vmap(pinn_a, in_axes=(None, 0, 0, 0))` (JAX; Lectures 11 and 13), where `None` keeps the network fixed and each `0` runs over the samples.

The loss is $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{data} + \mathcal{L}_\text{physics}$:
- $\mathcal{L}_\text{data}$: the mean squared error of `pinn_a` against every sample of experiments 1 to 4.
- $\mathcal{L}_\text{physics}$: the mean of $r_X^2 + r_S^2$ at the same points.

Train it, predict experiment 5 with `pinn_a(net_a, t, 3.0, 80.0)`, and plot the prediction with the measurements. How good is it? Why?

b) **A PINN that works.** Build a second PINN with four changes:
1. It describes experiment 5 only, so its only input is $t$.
2. Its data is the initial condition of experiment 5, and its physics loss is at 301 points from 0 to 300 s, `np.linspace(0, 300, 301)`. It uses no measurements. Its residuals are those of `residuals_a`, with `pinn_b(net, t)` in place of `pinn_a(net, t, X0, S0)`.
3. Its input and outputs are scaled to order one, and `jax.nn.softplus` (JAX; Lecture 13) keeps $X$ and $S$ positive:

```python
net_b = eqx.nn.MLP(            # t -> (X, S), for experiment 5 only
    in_size=1,
    out_size=2,
    width_size=32,
    depth=3,
    activation=jnp.tanh,
    key=jax.random.PRNGKey(0),
)


def pinn_b(net, t):
    # t / 300 runs from 0 to 1
    out = net(jnp.array([t / 300]))
    # X near 5 and S near 100, both positive
    return jnp.array([5.0, 100.0]) * jax.nn.softplus(out)
```

4. The losses are scaled the same way, and the physics is weighted by 0.01:
   - $\mathcal{L}_\text{data}$: the mean of the squared errors at $t = 0$, divided by $5^2$ for $X$ and $100^2$ for $S$.
   - $\mathcal{L}_\text{physics}$: the mean over the 301 points of $(300\,r_X/5)^2 + (300\,r_S/100)^2$.
   - $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{data} + 0.01\,\mathcal{L}_\text{physics}$.

Train it, evaluate `pinn_b` at $t = 0, 5, \ldots, 300$ s, and plot it with the measurements. How good is it? Which of the four changes do you think mattered most, and why? What would it take to predict a sixth experiment?

## 3. Neural ODE
Consider the same balances with two networks: $\mu_\text{NN}(S)$, the growth rate in the biomass balance, and $f_\text{NN}(X, S)$, the whole right-hand side of the substrate balance:

$$
\frac{dX}{dt} = -\frac{F}{V}\,X + \mu_\text{NN}(S)\,X, \qquad
\frac{dS}{dt} = f_\text{NN}(X, S)
$$

The biomass balance keeps its physics. Nothing of the substrate balance is given.

Using Equinox, Diffrax and Optax, as in `l13-neural-dae.ipynb`, do the following:

a) Code the two networks and the right-hand side:

```python
mu_net = eqx.nn.MLP(           # S -> growth rate mu
    in_size=1,
    out_size=1,
    width_size=16,
    depth=2,
    activation=jnp.tanh,
    key=jax.random.PRNGKey(0),
)
f_net = eqx.nn.MLP(            # (X, S) -> dS/dt
    in_size=2,
    out_size=1,
    width_size=16,
    depth=2,
    activation=jnp.tanh,
    key=jax.random.PRNGKey(1),
)
nets = (mu_net, f_net)


def mu_nn(mu_net, S):
    # growth rate (1/s): positive, and of the size of the true mu (at most 0.044)
    return 0.05 * jax.nn.softplus(mu_net(jnp.array([S / 100]))[0])


def balances(t, x, nets):
    mu_net, f_net = nets
    X, S = x
    # biomass balance, with mu learned
    dXdt = -F / V * X + mu_nn(mu_net, S) * X
    # substrate balance, learned whole
    dSdt = f_net(jnp.array([X / 5, S / 100]))[0]
    return jnp.array([dXdt, dSdt])
```

b) Simulate with `diffrax.diffeqsolve` (Diffrax; Lecture 13), using `diffrax.Tsit5()`, `dt0=0.5`, `diffrax.PIDController(rtol=1e-6, atol=1e-8)` and `args=nets`.

c) Train on experiments 1 to 4 for 10,000 steps, as in `l13-neural-dae.ipynb`: the loss is the mean of $\left((\text{prediction} - \text{data}) / \sigma\right)^2$, with $\sigma$ the standard deviation of their $X$ and of their $S$, and the optimizer is `optax.adam(optax.cosine_decay_schedule(1e-2, 10_000, alpha=0.05))` (Optax; Lecture 13). Train the pair `nets` the way the notebook trains its one network: `eqx.filter` and `eqx.filter_value_and_grad` accept a tuple of networks.

d) Predict experiment 5 by integrating from its initial condition, with no retraining. Plot it with the measurements.

e) Evaluate `mu_nn` at $S = 0, 10, \ldots, 300$ mol/m³ with `jax.vmap(mu_nn, in_axes=(None, 0))` (JAX; Lectures 11 and 13). Plot it with the true $\mu(S)$ on the same grid. Where do they agree? Where not, and why?
